## 📖 About This Text

This is a **multilingual liturgical text** containing the complete practice manual for the daily offering ritual to the Eight Medicine Buddhas (*Men-la de-shek gyay*). It is a concise yet profound ritual synthesis — described in its colophon as *"concise in words yet great in meaning"* — compiled for daily, uninterrupted practice.

The text presents every section in **four parallel languages**:

| Script | Language | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| དབུ་ཅན་ | Classical Tibetan | Original liturgical source |
| Wylie Transliteration | Phonetic Tibetan (Roman) | Pronunciation guide for non-Tibetan readers |
| English | Translation | Semantic understanding |
| 中文 | Chinese (Traditional/Simplified) | Chinese-speaking practitioners |

## 📿 Structure of the Ritual

The practice follows this sequence:

1. **Homage** — *Namo guru sarva bhagavanta tathāgata…*
2. **Refuge & Bodhicitta** — Taking refuge in Guru, Buddha, Dharma, Sangha
3. **Four Immeasurables** — Aspiration for all sentient beings
4. **Purification of the Environment** — By the power of the Three Jewels
5. **Invitation** — Summoning the Eight Sugatas and Śākyamuni
6. **Individual Homages** — To each of the Eight Medicine Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and dharma protectors
7. **Sevenfold Offering** — Physical and mentally-generated offerings, confession, rejoicing, requesting Dharma, requesting long presence
8. **Long Mantra** (སྔགས་རིང་)

   ```
   OM NAMO BHAGAVATE BHAIṢAJYAGURU VAIḌŪRYA PRABHĀRĀJĀYA
   TATHĀGATĀYA ARHATE SAMYAKSAMBUDDHĀYA TADYATHĀ:
   OM BHAIṢAJYE BHAIṢAJYE MAHĀBHAIṢAJYE
   BHAIṢAJYARĀJA SAMUDGATE SVĀHĀ
   ```

9. **Short Mantra** (སྔགས་ཐུང་)

   ```
   TADYATHĀ: OM BHAIṢAJYE BHAIṢAJYE MAHĀBHAIṢAJYE
   BHAIṢAJYARĀJA SAMUDGATE SVĀHĀ
   ```

10. **Dedication** — Merit dedication for swift accomplishment
11. **Auspicious Verses** — Six closing verses for the doctrine, practitioners, and all beings
12. **Aspiration for Beings** — Prayers for freedom from illness
13. **Dissolution** — Requesting wisdom beings to depart (*OM VAJRA MUḤ*)
14. **Final Auspicious Prayers**

## 🏔️ Lineage & Colophon

This text was composed within the **Sakya tradition** (*ས་སྐྱ་*):

- **Requested by:** Sönam Wangpo — heart son of the mantra-holder Dharma King *Ngawang Kunga Rinchen*
- **Inspired by:** The lord father, hidden Buddha and mantra-holder *Dragpa Lodrö* (emanation of Bodhisattva Śāntarakṣita)
- **Composed by:** *Ngawang Kunga Sönam*, Sakyapa lay yogi, at the *Ösel Nangwa* chamber of **Pal Sakya's Zhitog Labrang**, in the Dragon Year, 5th month
- **Scribe:** *Dong-gā-wa Sampa Dönchen*, expert in medical sciences and calligraphy
- **Occasion:** At the request of *Gön-sam*, a master of the science of longevity (Ayurveda), who needed a convenient daily practice ritual
- **Multilingual Edition Arranged by:** Khenpo Kunga Rinchen — expanded from abbreviated sources for the benefit of foreign practitioners

## 🌿 Practice Notes

> *"By practicing in this way daily without interruption, the benefits taught in the sūtras will definitely arise without a doubt. Therefore, those who desire virtues and qualities should strive in this profound essential point, which is concise in words yet great in meaning."*
>
> — Colophon of the text

- The **long and short dhāraṇīs** may be recited as many times as appropriate according to one's inclination
- When a **physical support** (statue/image) is present, offer stability prayers; when absent, recite the dissolution verse
- Conclude with whatever **dedication, aspiration, and auspicious prayers** are appropriate
